About us

We build tools that put learning within reach.

Visioners is a student-founded assistive technology company based at LBS College of Engineering, Kasaragod. We design hardware and software that give visually impaired students direct, independent access to printed learning material.

Mission

Make Braille learning material available instantly and affordably in every school, regardless of location or budget.

Vision

A classroom where no student is held back by the format of a book.

The problem

Blind students don’t lack talent. They lack access.

Textbooks are rarely available in Braille. Audio helps, but it cannot replace touch-based reading, which is what builds literacy, spelling and comprehension.

01

Dependence on others

Limited Braille material means students rely on teachers and peers for every new page.

02

Left behind in class

Study material arrives late, and fast-paced classrooms move on without them.

03

Priced out of reach

Imported Braille printers cost ₹3,00,000 or more and need digital input, which puts them out of reach for most rural schools and NGOs.

Our product

A Braille printer that reads for itself.

Place a printed page on the scanner. Optical character recognition reads it, a translation engine converts it to Braille, and the embosser raises the dots on paper.

01

Place

Lay any printed page on the scanner bed.

02

Read

OCR extracts the text from the page.

03

Translate

A Braille engine converts text into Braille cells.

04

Emboss

Raised dots are printed on specialised paper.

Learning you can feel

“The only thing worse than being blind is having sight but no vision.”

Helen Keller

Helen Keller learned language through touch, one word pressed into her hand at a time. Braille carries that same idea onto the page, and it is why we are building a machine that makes it instant.

Questions

What people ask us.

Does it need a computer or internet?

No. The device scans, reads and embosses on its own, so it works in schools without IT infrastructure.

Is it available to buy today?

Not yet. Our prototype is in development and we are validating it with schools. Write to us if you would like to take part in a pilot.

How much will it cost?

We are targeting about ₹1.5 lakh per unit, which is half the price of imported Braille printers. Rental plans and community kiosks are planned for those who cannot buy outright.

Who is it designed for?

Blind schools, government education institutes and NGOs, so that visually impaired students and their teachers get Braille material when they need it.

How can we work with you?

Schools can pilot the device, NGOs and CSR teams can help place units in underserved schools, and mentors can advise us. Use the form below or email us.
